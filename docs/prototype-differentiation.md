# Are the three prototypes really different?

Reserve, Instant and Value all answer the same question from the Define workbook: *will students hand their furniture to Relay for the May-to-August gap, and on what terms?* They differ in **who owns the furniture and who carries the risk** while it sits in storage. Brand, catalog and the pickup screens are held constant on purpose, so the vote measures the model, not the styling.

| | **01 Reserve** | **02 Instant** | **03 Value** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Persona | Ryan, incoming Fuqua student (buyer) | Allan, graduating undergrad who wants it gone (seller) | Nemo, PhD candidate who wants the best price (seller) |
| Who owns it | Relay holds and pre-sells | Relay buys it outright | Seller keeps title |
| Who carries the risk | Buyer (the delivery promise) | Relay (inventory) | Seller (price and time) |
| Seller or buyer gets | A guaranteed delivery window | One cash offer, paid at the door | The upside, after 10% commission and an $8 storage fee |
| The screen that decides it | Inspection report and date guarantee | Price drop at the door ($95 to $80) | Floor price and required Sept 15 fallback, then offer/counter |
| Hypothesis | Date certainty beats price | A seller with days left takes less for one pickup, even after the re-price | A seller will trade three months of waiting for a floor and a stated fallback |
| Wrong if | Buyers browse, then bail at reserve | Sellers accept the photo offer, then refuse at the door | Sellers set a floor, see no offers, then ask to be bought out |
| What to measure | Reservations before arrival; % picking the guaranteed date | Offer acceptance; door re-price acceptance (target above 70%) | Listing completion; % setting a floor above the instant price; fallback chosen; offer response |

## Where the readings help

- **Running Lean (desirability, viability, feasibility; product, customer, market risk).** Each prototype attacks a different riskiest assumption. Reserve tests customer desire to commit early. Instant tests whether the instant, outright-purchase economics survive contact with the seller (cost of $95 against a $220 resale in the Ops numbers). Value tests whether sellers want control enough to wait, and moves inventory risk off Relay. This mapping is our reading of the framework, not the book's wording.
- **Swanson Ch 4 (market pull vs technology push).** All three serve a market that already exists (students move every May). None is technology push. The difference is the business model, which is what a vote should measure.
- **Week 4 (hypothesis-driven discovery).** Each prototype names a falsifiable hypothesis and a result that would prove it wrong, as above.

## Honest overlap to defend in the presentation

Instant and Value share the seller persona base, the photo/category/condition screen, and the pickup map. That is intentional (held constant), but a grader will look for what is genuinely different. In the demo, skip the shared screens and spend the time on the moments that differ: **the door re-price** (Instant) and **the floor, fallback and counter-offer dashboard** (Value).

`/ops` (Relay Ops, the internal stock-in/stock-out view) is the fourth card on the hub. It is an internal tool rather than a customer-facing option, so frame it in the presentation as the operations view that supports whichever model wins, not as a fourth thing to vote on.
